# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Ri'ayah has seven actor groups, each with exactly one job on screen.

The primary audience is students: university students (mahasiswa) and, after gate G3, school children and teenagers (SD, SMP, SMA). They open the app on a phone, often alone, sometimes while already feeling worn down. Their job is to understand and control what the system reads about them, answer an optional weekly check-in, read why they were flagged, and reach a human when they want one.

The secondary audiences are the helpers and guardians around them. Homeroom teachers and academic advisors (guru wali kelas, dosen PA) greet yellow-zone students on a desktop between classes. Counselors (guru BK, konselor) work a red and yellow queue against response deadlines. Parents or guardians (school only, phone) give consent, receive counselor invitations, and exercise data rights for their child. School admins or IT run imports, relations, rules, and emergency contacts. Leadership reads aggregate statistics only. The ethics committee and DPO audit logs, fairness, objections, and the rule registry.

## Product Purpose

Ri'ayah helps a campus or school greet a student who is showing early signs of strain before the problem shows up in grades. It reads four engagement indicators the institution already holds (attendance, online learning activity, late assignments, quiz trend) plus an optional check-in, and produces one of three zones. Every flag carries at most three plain-language reasons and one note on what would change it. A human always decides the next step.

Success means every yellow flag is greeted personally within seven days, every red is contacted within 24 hours, every student can read and contest their flag, and screening data is never used for grading, sanctions, selection, or scholarships. Stopping the system is a legitimate outcome if evidence shows harm.

## Positioning

Ri'ayah is a door that brings a student to a human who cares, and never replaces that human.

## Brand Personality

Calm, honest, easy to decline. The voice is that of a trusted homeroom teacher speaking quietly: short sentences, everyday words, no labels. It invites and explains rather than detects or commands ("Mau cerita ke Bu Sari?", not "Kamu wajib ke BK"). Every request for data or a greeting has a "Nanti" or "Belum mau" of equal weight. The emotional goal for a student is "I am in control and nobody is judging me"; for staff it is "I know who to greet, why, and by when".

Islamic grounding is structural, not decorative: ta'awun (helping through people), the prohibition of tajassus (no prying, no passive sensing), and amanah (data held in trust). Islamic resources are always optional.

## Anti-references

- Surveillance and monitoring dashboards: risk scores, red heat maps of students, leaderboards, "pantau" language.
- Clinical or diagnostic apps: "pasien", "gangguan", "berisiko", probability percentages on a student's screen.
- Gamified wellness apps with streaks, confetti, and nudging that punishes declining.
- Generic SaaS admin templates with hero metrics and identical card grids.
- Sad-face empty states and alarm-red everywhere.

## Design Principles

1. **Human decides.** The interface surfaces reasons and routes to a person; it never presents itself as the decision.
2. **Show what is seen and what is not.** Every screen that touches data says what the system reads and what it never reads. Simulated data is labelled SIMULASI.
3. **Declining is free.** Refusal paths carry equal visual weight and no consequence, and the screen says so.
4. **Same explanation for everyone.** Student and teacher read the same reasons in the same words; depth differs by role, never the story.
5. **Help is always one tap away.** "Butuh bantuan sekarang" is on every student screen, including offline and while loading.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA as the floor: body text 4.5:1, UI components 3:1, touch targets at least 44 by 44 px with 8 px spacing, full-width help button. A high-contrast mode is available in the student privacy screen. Zone is never conveyed by color alone (text plus icon plus shape). Trend charts have text summaries. Reduced motion is respected and transitions are limited to 150 to 250 ms state changes. Language is short and plain, adjusted to school level; child mode (SD, early SMP) has no zones, numbers, charts, or the word "ditandai". Indonesian is the primary language with text structured for regional languages later.
